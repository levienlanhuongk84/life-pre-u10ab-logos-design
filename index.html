Aims of the lesson & Lead-in

Aims of the lesson

After the lesson, students will be able to:

📚Kiến thức
  • explain key vocabulary used to describe product design;
  • describe the way passive voice changes the focus of meaning in a sentence;
  • compare used to with other expressions of past habits.
🛠️Kỹ năng
  • demonstrate correct use of the Passive Voice in writing tasks;
  • practise using related word forms in production and advertising tasks;
  • perform clear speaking and writing tasks to express ideas on product topics.
💡Thái độ
  • appreciate the cultural and economic significance of local products and logos;
  • value the role of design and innovation in improving people's daily lives.

Ngữ pháp của bài

The passive · SB tr. 119

🔧Dạng
  • be + quá khứ phân từ (V3)
  • thì nằm ở be: is / are (hiện tại) · was / were (quá khứ)
  • V3 bất quy tắc hay gặp: made · sold · put · bought · built · written
  • Apple products are used in millions of homes.
  • The first laptops were produced in 1999.
🎯Dùng khi nào
  • không biết hoặc không quan tâm ai làm
  • muốn nói về sản phẩm, không nói về người làm
  • chủ ngữ = vật nhận hành động
  • The logo was turned round. (ai quay không quan trọng)
  • Over 500 iPhones are sold every minute.
👤Nêu tác nhân với by
  • thêm by + người / công ty khi thông tin đó quan trọng
  • so sánh: People recognize an Apple product (chủ động) → An Apple product is recognized by people
  • An Apple product is recognized by people all over the world.
  • The Walkman was invented by Sony.

used to · SB tr. 121

⏮Dạng
  • used to + động từ nguyên thể
  • phủ định: didn't use to + V (bỏ d)
  • câu hỏi: Did you use to + V?
  • People used to buy music on vinyl records.
  • We didn't use to download music.
  • Did you use to buy CDs?
🕰Nghĩa
  • thói quen hoặc trạng thái trong quá khứ
  • bây giờ không còn đúng nữa
  • thường đi với nowadays / these days / but now ở vế sau
  • My mother used to have a Walkman, but now she streams everything.
  • I didn't use to like vegetables — I love them now.
⚡used to hay past simple?
  • hành động một lần, thời điểm cụ thể → quá khứ đơn
  • thói quen lặp lại → used to
  • phát âm used to = /juːstə/ (âm /s/, không đọc d)
  • phát âm động từ use = /juːz/
  • He bought his first record in 1973. (một lần)
  • He used to buy a record every week. (thói quen)
Khung hay dùng khi thi

I used to …, whereas now I tend to … because ….

I used to buy CDs every month, whereas now I tend to stream music because it's more convenient.

5 câu bị động về sản phẩm của em

Đổi bài với bạn — bạn có đoán được sản phẩm là gì không?

8 chức năng · 24 chunks

1 · Nêu ý kiến

  • In my opinion, …
  • I'd say (that) …
  • Personally, I think …

Personally, I think a logo should be simple enough to draw from memory.

2 · Lý do

  • The main reason is that …
  • …, mainly because …
  • That's why …

I chose this brand mainly because it has built up a reputation for quality.

3 · Ví dụ

  • For instance, …
  • Take …, for example.
  • A good example of this is …

Take the Apple logo, for example — it's instantly recognizable on any laptop.

4 · Tả sản phẩm

  • It's a … with … / It looks …
  • What makes it special is (that) …
  • It's sleek / minimalist / built to last

What makes it special is that it's both ergonomic and eye-catching.

5 · Xuất xứ & công dụng (bị động)

  • It's used for + V-ing
  • It was designed / invented by …
  • It's made in … and sold …

It was designed by a small Danish company and is now sold all over the world.

6 · Xưa và nay

  • People used to …, but nowadays …
  • Back then, … / These days, …
  • … has been replaced by …

People used to buy cassettes, but nowadays streaming has replaced them completely.

7 · So sánh – đối lập

  • whereas / while
  • compared to …
  • On the other hand, …

Local products are cheaper, whereas international brands are easier to trust.

8 · Nhượng bộ – kết luận

  • Admittedly, … However, …
  • Although …
  • On balance, … / All in all, …

Admittedly, branded products are expensive. On balance, though, they're good value for money.

Khung trả lời 5 bước

Câu hỏi mẫu: "Should companies change their logos over time?"

1Point

Only when there's a good reason.

2Why

because a logo is how customers recognize the company.

3Example

When Gap changed its logo in 2010, loyal customers complained and it was changed back within a week.

4Counter

Admittedly, an old-fashioned logo can make a brand look obsolete.

5Evaluation

On balance, small updates are fine; a completely new logo is a risk.

Dùng ở đâu
  • Part 1: Point + Why (+ Example) — 2–3 mệnh đề.
  • Part 2: theo 4 ý của cue card, mỗi ý = Point + Why hoặc Example.
  • Part 2: kết bằng một câu Evaluation.
  • Part 3 và đoạn văn: đủ 5 bước, không bỏ Counter và Evaluation.

Reading B · What makes a design classic?

What makes a design classic?

1Every year thousands of new products go on sale, and within a few months most of them are forgotten. A handful, though, last for decades: the Coca-Cola bottle, the Vespa scooter, the Sony Walkman. Designers call these "design classics". What do they actually have in common?

2The first thing they share is that they are usually simple. The Walkman, launched in 1979, did exactly one job: it played cassettes, and it did that job well. That simplicity is probably why it succeeded, and even now, long after cassette players became obsolete, most people can still picture its shape.

3The second quality is that a classic is user-friendly: nobody needs an instruction manual to ride a Vespa or switch on an Anglepoise lamp, because the design itself tells you what to do. A classic is also built to last. Plenty of products are mass-produced from cheap plastic and end up in the bin as soon as a newer model appears; a real classic is made of durable materials, and some even look better with age.

4Above all, a genuine classic changes the way people actually live. Before the Walkman, listening to music meant sitting at home next to a stereo; afterwards, it was something you carried around with you. That is what makes a product a game-changer: once it exists, people find it hard to imagine daily life without it.

5Not every attractive product becomes a classic, of course. Fashionable designs go out of fashion, and eye-catching colours can look old-fashioned within a couple of years. The objects that last are rarely the most exciting ones; they are simply the ones that keep doing their job quietly, year after year.

On your way to school or work this morning, you probably listened to music on your headphones. Maybe you downloaded your favourite music onto a device which holds thousands of songs, or played your favourite songs through your phone. These days, listening to music is a personal activity that we all do on our own, but it didn't use to be so easy to get music or to listen when you were on the move. In the seventies, people used to buy music on vinyl records and play them on record players at home.

But in 1979, the Sony Walkman changed the way people listened to music. In the eighties and nineties, you used to see people everywhere with their Sony Walkman, a few cassettes and a set of headphones on. In the end Sony's product was so successful that other companies copied the idea, but Sony's original Walkman was always the most popular. By 1986 the name Walkman was included as a word in the English dictionary.

Looking back, the idea of the Walkman seems so simple, but simplicity was the reason for its success. At the time, some people thought it was a crazy idea; after all, who wanted a music player with no radio, no speakers, no way to record, and small headphones? They were wrong, of course. The Walkman did everything people wanted: it was small enough to carry, it played music, and it was personal.

Writing 2 · Đoạn văn 120–150 từ

Đề · chọn 1 trong 3 (từ bộ đề nói)
  • Đề 1 · Topic 2: Describe a brand logo that you like — what it looks like, which company it represents, where you see it, and why you like it.
  • Đề 2 · Topic 10: Describe a product you saw that was beautifully designed — what it was, where you saw it, what it looked like, and why you think it was beautifully designed.
  • Đề 3 · Topic 1: Describe a product that you would like to buy — what it is, when you want to buy it, its special features, and why you want it.

Write one paragraph of 120–150 words. Use at least five topic collocations, one passive sentence and one sentence with used to.

Muốn viết về sản phẩm của chính mình? Lấy nội dung thẳng từ My Product Dossier (→ Sheet 3).

Bố cục 1 đoạn · 5 câu nhóm
  • Câu chủ đề: nêu sản phẩm / logo + 1 tính từ đánh giá — The most beautifully designed product I have ever seen is …
  • Ý 1 — hình thức: 2 tính từ Group 2 + chất liệu (is made of)
  • Ý 2 — xuất xứ / nơi thấy: bị động — It was designed by … / It is sold …
  • Ý 3 — vì sao thích: lý do + so sánh xưa nay (used to … but nowadays …)
  • Câu kết: đánh giá — All in all, … / On balance, …
Ngôn ngữ cho đoạn
  • Mở: The product I'd like to describe is … / One logo I really like is …
  • Tả: sleek · minimalist · ergonomic · eye-catching · built to last
  • Xuất xứ: was designed by · is manufactured in · went on sale in · is widely used
  • Lý do: What I like most about it is (that) … / The main reason is that …
  • Kết: All in all, it's good value for money and has stood the test of time.
Bài mẫu · đề 2 (≈ 147 từ)

PointThe most beautifully designed product I have ever seen is the Littmann stethoscope that my supervisor uses. HowIt is sleek and minimalist: the tube is dark blue, the chest piece is made of polished steel, and the earpieces are ergonomic, so they are comfortable on long shifts. ExampleThe stethoscope was designed by an American cardiologist, David Littmann, in the 1960s, and today it is sold worldwide. WhyWhat I like most about it is the attention to detail — even the small ring on the tube is engraved with the brand name. Student stethoscopes used to be heavy and old-fashioned, but this one is light and clearly built to last: my supervisor has used hers for fifteen years. EvaluationAdmittedly, it is not affordable for most students. All in all, though, it is good value for money, because a doctor uses it every day.

Mercury thermometers were used in every hospital for more than a hundred years. They were cheap, accurate and easy to read. However, they are no longer sold in most countries because mercury is dangerous if the glass breaks. Digital thermometers, which give a reading in ten seconds, have replaced them.

My phone is a Samsung Galaxy, which I bought two years ago. It's sleek and minimalist — just a black glass rectangle — but it isn't durable: the screen was cracked after one fall. Mẫu

The digital blood-pressure monitor is used for checking a patient's blood pressure at home. It is made of light plastic, and the manufacturer, Omron, is a household name in medical equipment. Nurses used to measure blood pressure with a mercury monitor, but those have been phased out because mercury is dangerous. This model is user-friendly — there is only one button — and it is affordable, so it is widely used by older patients. You put the cuff on your arm, press the button and wait twenty seconds. Admittedly, it is less accurate than a doctor's reading; on balance, though, it has changed the way patients look after their own health.
